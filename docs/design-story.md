# DNA Armour Homepage Design Story

## Narrative

The opening sequence introduces the front of the product, turns to the brand promise, then reveals the formula. The motion follows the physical bottle while the language moves from identity to benefit to proof.

## Emotional journey

1. Curiosity: a large isolated bottle and the line “Protect your prime.”
2. Relevance: a simple, compliant statement about tiredness and fatigue.
3. Confidence: exact serving and ingredient information from the approved label.
4. Simplicity: the closing ritual reduces the formula to two capsules once a day.

## Peak moments

- Continuous scroll-controlled rotation of one 3D bottle through three readable views
- The dark typographic statement section
- The cyan formula card with the large NR+ orbital device

## Source of truth

Artwork, nutritional values, serving details, caution wording and the health claim are taken from the replacement `DNA Label.pdf`, copied to `assets/dna-label.pdf`. The earlier `NR Label.pdf` and generated product photos are no longer referenced by the page. The replacement lists the same nutrient quantities as the earlier PDF, so those table values remain unchanged.

The source PDF is rendered at 4096 pixels wide. Only its bottom white print margin is omitted from the cylinder texture. Label text is not regenerated. The model is a visual approximation based on the reference bottle, not a manufacturing model.

The bottle mesh is widened by 20% to match the front-view reference more closely. “Nicotinamide Riboside” is the opening hero title and reappears as the first row of the scroll-driven ingredient reel, creating a continuous handoff between the first and second hero states.

The navigation now centres the experience around Home, The Science, and Discover NR+. The science section presents familiar ingredients first, uses restrained transparent line illustrations, and links each explanation to NIH, NCCIH, or a human-evidence review. `shop.html` is the visual purchase page for one-off and 60-day subscription choices; pricing and a payment provider are still required to make checkout transactional.
